# Whatte visual identity

The approved visual direction for Whatte product pages is the editorial
language established by the [Whatte Today prototype](https://whatte-today-editorial.shchukins.chatgpt.site)
(private reference). New pages and future redesigns should use the same
language. This document does not by itself authorize redesigning an existing
page or changing its behavior.

## Design rules

- Use a light, warm, paper-like background and near-black text.
- Use an editorial serif for major findings and section headlines; a clear
  sans-serif for body text, controls, and explanations; monospace for dates,
  metadata, labels, figures, and technical values.
- Use muted olive, ochre, brick, and slate accents sparingly. Never use a
  bright fitness-app palette, gradients, glass effects, neon, or decorative
  circular gauges.
- Functional score controls may use a discrete muted green-to-ochre-to-brick
  color scale. Keep selection visible through borders as well as color.
- Build hierarchy with generous whitespace, asymmetric columns where useful,
  thin rules, and type. Avoid stacks of rounded cards and heavy shadows.
- Lead with an observation or decision. Place the stored metric and evidence
  nearby as support. Keep figures minimal, labeled, and captioned.
- Keep CLI influence limited to small metadata and technical labels; do not
  make the interface look like a terminal.
- Keep controls readable, keyboard accessible, and usable on narrow screens.
  Preserve missing and stale states rather than filling gaps with sample data.

## Product boundary

The visual style changes presentation only. Product pages render backend-owned
readiness, recommendation, and explanation. They must not infer training
decisions from chart shape, freshness, or frontend calculations. Illustrative
claims and numbers in the prototype are not product data or model rules.

## Rollout

Web Today and its dated profile page use this identity. The profile reuses
Today’s palette and typography in a compact, centered layout (760px maximum)
with inline form fields on desktop and stacked fields on narrow screens.
Use this identity when working on other pages, including Analytics, in their
own scoped changes. Analytics remains outside this rollout.
